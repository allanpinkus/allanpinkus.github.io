

Case No. 955 / 1918   Marriage contract of Dr. Ármin Steiner and his wife, born Györgyike Guttmann
(duplicate copy) 

Notarial Deed   

Before Béla Gáspár, Royal Notary Public of Budapest,   appeared today: 
Dr. Ármin Steiner, entrepreneur, and his wife, born Györgyike Guttmann, 
residents of Budapest, whose identities [were verified by] Dr. Jenő Zoltán, attorney-at-law 
(residing at 11 Kálmán Street, District V) and Ignácz Schmergel, inspector (residing at 10 
Korona Street, District V), residents of Budapest, personally known to me, acting simultaneously 
as identity witnesses and transaction witnesses; and in the continuous and joint presence 
of these transaction witnesses throughout the course of this legal act, declaring before 
me their testamentary dispositions upon death, they concluded the following   

1. Dr. Ármin Steiner and Györgyike Guttmann having entered into marriage in Budapest on the 23rd 
day of April, 1918, before the Registrar of District V of Budapest, the spouses hereby reaffirm 
their vow to treat one another with love and fidelity, to live together in cohabitation, and to 
support each other with sincere devotion under all circumstances of their lives.  

2. Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, brought into the marriage concluded with her husband 
a cash dowry of 200,000 — two hundred thousand Kronen — from her own assets. This cash dowry remains 
the exclusive property of Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, even with the knowledge of the 
community/society, and Dr. Ármin Steiner is entitled solely to the right of usufruct (use) thereof, 
with full freedom of management.   

3. Dr. Ármin Steiner [acknowledges/deposits] this 200,000 — two hundred thousand Kronen — cash dowry 
by means of a [passbook/certificate] issued by the Hungarian Commercial Bank of Pest under 
No. 106.125 for 200,000 Kronen passbook issued to Dr. Ármin Steiner, the receipt of which in cash 
he hereby acknowledges and confirms.   

4. Dr. Ármin Steiner gives to his wife, born Györgyike Guttmann, from his own assets a wedding gift 
(parapherna) of 20,000 — twenty thousand — Kronen, which Mrs. Ármin Steiner hereby accepts. 
From this day forward, this wedding gift constitutes the exclusive property of Mrs. Ármin Steiner, 
born Györgyike Guttmann, and she alone is entitled to dispose of it freely. Should a dispute arise 
between the spouses regarding the marriage, or should the securing of this wedding gift—or its release 
from the husband's assets—become necessary due to divorce or any other reason, Dr. Ármin Steiner 
has no right to reclaim this wedding gift from his wife's assets; it is to be considered as 
fulfilling all potential property claims of the wife, and is therefore non-refundable and entirely irrevocable.   

5. Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, in addition to her 200,000 Kronen dowry mentioned 
in Clause 2 of this marriage contract  also brought into the house of her husband, Dr. Ármin Steiner, 
a trousseau (kelengye) fitting her bourgeois social standing—consisting of clothing, linens, bedding, 
tablecloths, and all supplementary items itemized in a signed inventory—valued at 15,000 (fifteen thousand) 
Kronen and 80 (eighty) Fillér, which remains the exclusive property of Mrs. Ármin Steiner, 
born Györgyike Guttmann. Insofar as these chattels were intended by the spouses for joint use, 
Dr. Ármin Steiner is entitled solely to the right of use. Dr. Ármin Steiner acknowledges that he has 
taken over the items intended for joint use in intact condition as listed in the aforementioned inventory, 
and that the value of these chattels amounts to 11,467 (eleven thousand four hundred sixty-seven) 
Kronen and 18 (eighteen) Fillér.   

6. The spouses agree that there shall be no community of property (vagyonközösség) between them, 
and that during the existence of the marriage, the assets acquired by the husband—which are exclusively 
the husband's property—shall not be subject to any joint ownership claims by the wife. Dr. Ármin Steiner 
declares that his current assets—consisting of real estate, business ventures, securities, and 
receivables—amount to 700,000 (seven hundred thousand) Kronen.   

7. In the event that a disagreement arises between the spouses for any reason, rendering further 
marital cohabitation impossible, and Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, leaves her husband, 
or Dr. Ármin Steiner terminates their cohabitation, Dr. Ármin Steiner is obligated—until a legally 
binding court judgment is issued dissolving the marriage—to pay in advance on the first day of 
every month provisional maintenance (ideiglenes tartásdíj) to his wife in the amount of 
500 (five hundred) Kronen, and for any children born of the marriage 100 (one hundred) Kronen per 
child, under penalty of immediate enforcement pursuant to § 114 of Act XXXI of 1894. The established 
child support applies in the event that the child or children remain under the care of Mrs. Ármin 
Steiner; no child support is due for any child cared for by the father. The final amount of spousal 
and child maintenance shall be determined by a legally binding court judgment. Regarding the provisional 
spousal and child maintenance, the litigating party is entitled to seek modification of the established 
amount through legal channels in view of changed financial or economic circumstances.   

8. In the event that the marriage is dissolved without children born of the marriage, Dr. Ármin Steiner 
is obligated to pay back and hand over the 200,000 Kronen dowry as well as the 20,000 Kronen wedding gift 
to his wife in three equal installments: namely, 1/3 (one-third) within eight days after the termination 
of marital cohabitation, another 1/3 (one-third) after three months, and the remaining final one-third (1/3) 
after a further three months—likewise under penalty of immediate enforcement pursuant to § 114 of the aforementioned 
Act. Furthermore, the chattels constituting the property of Mrs. Ármin Steiner under Clause 5 above, insofar 
as they still exist, shall be taken back in kind by Mrs. Ármin Steiner. Insofar as the items intended for 
joint use no longer exist in kind, Dr. Ármin Steiner is obligated to reimburse their itemized unit value 
as established in the inventory in cash.   Should children be born of the marriage, only half of the wedding gift 
and dowry shall be payable to Mrs. Ármin Steiner within the deadlines established above, while the other half 
shall be secured as maternal inheritance (anyai örökség) in equal shares for each child—with the provision 
that in this case, the entire wedding gift shall belong to Mrs. Ármin Steiner and her children whereas the 
dowry shall only revert to the children to the extent of half its amount. The refundable amount due to the 
children shall be retained by Dr. Ármin Steiner in installments of 1/3 — 1/3 (one-third, one-third) at each 
respective due date, and he is obligated to deposit it into the Guardianship Authority (árvaszéki pénztár) of 
the competent court on the day of its maturity. The interest on the deposited sum shall be used to settle 
child support or applied toward the maintenance of the child. Should any child pass away before 
Mrs. Ármin Steiner, that child's share shall be released in favor of Mrs. Ármin Steiner.   

9. In the event of death, while expressly reserving the right of free testamentary disposition, the spouses agree as follows:   
    a) If children are born of the marriage, the rules of Hungarian statutory inheritance law shall apply.   
    b) If the spouses' marriage remains childless and Mrs. Ármin Steiner is the survivor, then if his wife, born Györgyike Guttmann, [is widowed]
	within three (3) years calculated from the date of the marriage's conclusion, Dr. Ármin Steiner is obligated to hand over to his wife's 
	statutory heirs the full amount of the dowry; within the third to sixth year of the marriage, half of that amount; whereas in the 
	event of death occurring after six years, the entire dowry shall remain the property of Dr. Ármin Steiner—or should he also pass away, 
	it shall pass exclusively to his own blood relatives, excluding his wife's separate estate, while his wife's own acquired and 
	inherited assets and her clothing shall pass to her statutory heirs, waiving any further mutual claims.   


Should Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, be the survivor, then Mrs. Ármin Steiner shall inherit from Dr. Ármin Steiner's 
separate estate—to the exclusion of her husband's blood relatives and waiving any further statutory inheritance claims—if Dr. 
Ármin Steiner passes away within two (2) years calculated from the date of the marriage's conclusion, in addition to 
the wedding gift established in Clause 4  50,000 (fifty thousand) Kronen; between the third and sixth year of the marriage, 
150,000 (one hundred fifty thousand) Kronen; whereas in the event of death occurring after six (6) years, 200,000 (two hundred thousand) 
Kronen, in addition to the wedding gift established in Clause 4.   

10. In the event that the husband goes bankrupt during the existence of the marriage, the wife or her legal successors 
may demand the return or securing of the dowry and the items listed in the attached inventory, as well as the 
immediate surrender of the wedding gift and the trousseau belonging to the wife.   

11. For any potential disputes arising from this contract, the contracting parties submit to the jurisdiction of the Royal Court of Budapest.   

It is noted that the contracting parties inserted one word ("három" / three) in the second line of page 9, and one word and 
one number ("két (2)" / two) in the last line of page 9.   

I, the undersigned Royal Notary, read aloud and explained this notarial deed to the contracting parties—who were continuously and 
jointly present throughout the entire legal act—in the presence of the transaction witnesses simultaneously acting as identity 
witnesses; and after the contracting parties declared before me and the witnesses, following clear and explicit questioning, 
that they had understood it, approved its contents as fully corresponding to their intentions and declared will, and ratified it, 
they signed it with their own hands before me together with the witnesses.  

I, the acting Royal Notary, attest that all requirements set forth in § 82 of Act XXXV of 1874 were fully complied with in the 
preparation of this deed. I further attest that Mrs. Ármin Steiner, born Györgyike Guttmann, handed over to her husband, 
Dr. Ármin Steiner, in my presence, the passbook issued by the Hungarian Commercial Bank of Pest under No. 106.125 for 
200,000 (two hundred thousand) Kronen referred to in Clause 3 of this deed, and that the latter took possession 
of said passbook in my presence.   

Done in Budapest, on the 25th day of April in the year 1918.
