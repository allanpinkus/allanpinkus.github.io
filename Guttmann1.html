Guttmann Family


    1. Introduction:

    This is a story about a portion of the Guttmann family. We start with Isaac Guttmann and Katherine Leinkauf Guttmann. But then we mainly follow the line of Katherine's biological sons Hermann Eisner Guttmann and Alfred Eisner Guttmann. We relate what we have learnt about the business dealings of Isaac Guttmann, and about the firms "Guttmann and Frank". And then add some comments and references with regards to the other parts of the family. Many details of the story remain obscure. As with most family histories reconstructed from a few surviving documents, the record is incomplete and often raises as many questions as it answers. But we were struck by how much we did uncover and discover. Please feel free to contact us if you can add to or correct anything herein.


    2. Isaac and Katherine:

    Isaac Guttmann was born in Oct 1840 in the town of Reca which is near Senec. Senec is about 30 km east of Pressburg. Pressburg was the German name for the city of Bratislava, Slovakia. This entire area was, until WWI, in the Hungarian part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. The Jews of this area spoke German, Yiddish and perhaps also Hungarian. Isaac was the son of Moses Guttmann (abt 1795 – 1874 Rete, Slovakia) and Miriam Regner Guttmann (1810 – Feb 10, 1900 Rete, Slovakia). Isaac first married Regina Blau (1843 – 1887) on 8 Aug 1862 in Dunaszerdahely (about 50 km southeast of Pressburg), see their marriage registration. Regina and Isaac had two daughters. Julie, who was born on 25 Aug 1866 and Hermine (Hani), who was born on 26 Nov 1869. Both were born in Dunaszerdahely. From what we know Isaac and Regina's families seem to have been traditional Orthodox Jews. The story that was told in the family was that Regina died young, leading Isaac to remarry. While it is true that Regina died while fairly young, that is aged 44, (see her death record) the fact is that Isaac and Regina divorced. This is noted both on Isaac’s second marriage record (see here) and on Regina’s death record. It seems that this was a family secret. We do not know the reason for the divorce. But note that the daughters were in the care of Isaac. So perhaps she had a mental breakdown or terrible postnatal depression. We can only speculate.

    Katherine (Katalin) Leinkauf was born in 1836 in Pressburg. Katherine was, from we can determine, one of six children of Hermann Leinkauf and Bella Weiss (abt 1796, Floss, Germany – 9 Dec 1852, Pressburg). Four of Katherine's siblings emigrated from Pressburg to Mobile, Alabama, over some years in the mid 19th century. They had an uncle Abraham Weiss who had previously emigrated from Floss to Mobile. Katherine and a sister named Minna remained in Pressburg. On 12 June 1864 Katherine married Max Eisner in Ganserndof, which is also near Pressburg, (see their marriage record). Max Eisner was born about 1828 in Pressburg. We have no details as to when he died, but it was before 1874. Katherine and Max had two sons, namely Hermann, born 21 Mar 1865 in Vienna, and Alfred, born 19 Feb 1866, also in Vienna.

    In May of 1874, in Pressburg, the widow Katherine Leinkauf Eisner married the divorced Isaac Guttmann, (see their marriage record). The marriage was conducted at the Neolog (or conservative) synagogue in Pressburg. Marrying in a Neolog synagogue seems a significant break from Orthodox tradition. But this was becoming more and more common as Neolog Judaism appealed primarily to middle-class, urbanized, and culturally integrated Hungarian Jews. Katherine and Isaac had together two daughters, namely Berta born on 7 Apr 1875 in Pressburg, and Malvina born on 7 Oct 1876 also in Pressburg. In addition Isaac officially adopted Katherine's two sons in 1894, and thus their family name also became Guttmann. Katherine and Isaac raised all 6 children, i.e., Katherine's two boys with Max Eisner, Isaac's two daughters with Regina Blau, and their two joint daughters.

    The emancipation of the Jews of the Austro-Hungary empire took place in 1867 and gave the Jews freedom to own property and to choose where to live. This is undoubtedly of importance in the Guttmann family story. At some point in time, probably in the 1880s, Isaac and Katherine moved to Budapest which became their primary residence. Isaac seems to have had various businesses connected in different ways with the construction industry. His main company was "I. Guttmann" that later became "I. Guttmann and Sons" ("I. Guttmann és Fiai"). This change of name probably occurred in conjunction with Isaac officially adopting Hermann and Alfred in 1894. (Hermann and Alfred were definitely involved in the company after 1895.) However there were also other companies and various partnerships. More about his businesses and projects below.

    Isaac died in Budapest on 4 Jan 1906 at the age of 65, (see here). An announcement of his death appeared in Pester Lloyd a German-language daily newspaper published in Budapest, (see here and here). When Isaac died, Isaac and Katherine were living at Váci Street 6, a prime location in the middle of Budapest, close to the Danube river.

    Katherine died of a stroke on 22 Mar 1915 in Budapest at the age of 79, (see here). Katherine and Isaac are buried in the Kozma Street Jewish cemetery in Budapest. In 2025 the grave was restored by descendants, namely by Michelle Dyson and her family.

    In 1903 Isaac and Katherine had prepared a joint will. The will was written in German. A (deputy) Royal Notary came to the Guttmann residence at Váci Street 2, ground floor (before moving to Váci Street 6). They gave him the will, jointly signed by them and by two witnesses. The notary deposited the will in a sealed envelope. The notary then read aloud the protocol to them and "since they do not command the Hungarian language, I, being authorized to record documents in the German language, translated it into German, and after the clients declared that it fully agreed with their intentions, it was signed by their own hands." From this we understand that at least Katherine, and perhaps also Isaac, were not fluent in Hungarian.

    When Isaac died in 1906 the will was then opened. This will can be found online here. Here is an English translation thereof. In the will all their property was left to the surviving spouse (in this case Katherine), except for certain legacies that were immediately distributed. Katherine died in 1915. The will stated that after the death of the remaining spouse the estate was to be divided equally among their 6 children, after a further number of legacies were distributed. These legacies were interesting for a number of reasons. When the will was written, in 1903, it seems that the two joint daughters of Isaac and Katherine, namely Berta and Malvine, were financially not in as good a position as their other children, despite their marriages. As such they were each bequeathed 50,000 Kronen, as a special pre-legacy. Note that 50,000 Kronen (25,000 Florin), was in 1903 the equivalent of between $3 to $6 million dollars in today's buying power. However by 1915, if the estate was still intact, it was worth some 30% less. The other legacies were to other relatives and Jewish charities.


    3. Isaac Guttmann and his Businesses:

    In the "Budpaest City Archives" and elsewhere are to be found many files with material relevant to the Guttmann family. We have so far found about 60 such file names, and at present we have access to the full files of about 1/3 of them. Many of these files have to do with notary deeds and contracts associated with Isaac Guttmann. However the period covered only starts from about 1891, when Isaac was in his 50's. By this time he seems to have amassed considerable capital and expertize. We do not know how, where or when. We review below what we know at present.

    In various Budapest phone directories we find both "Guttmann I. és Fiai" and "Guttmann J. és Fiai". (In Hungarian "script" at the time the capital "I" and "J" were indistinguishable.) However this was printed, so we do not understand why there are two listings. However they are the same company with the same three partners listed, namely Issac (Izsak), Hermann and Alfred, and they are all listed at Arany Janos 20, Budapest, with the same phone number. It is not entirely clear to us if this was a residence or a business office. Probably the latter. In these phone books they are listed as "Coal Dealers" (in the directories of 1896, 1904, 1905) as "Coal Mine Owners" (in the directories of 1898, 1900, 1902, 1903, 1904 and 1905), under the category "Danube Regulation Company" (in the directories of 1899 and 1900), as "Construction Contractors" (in the directories of 1902, 1903), and as being in the "Forest Industry" and "Timber Merchants" (in the directories of 1902, 1903, and 1904). See here.

    We know that Isaac, before making Hermann and Alfred his partners, had a steam brick factory in Budapest. ("Steam brick" refers to an industrial brickmaking plant powered by steam engines.) Preliminary construction and land development for the "Guttmann Brick Factory" began in 1891. Isaac Guttmann was officially issued his industrial trade license on July 8, 1892. The plant was sold to a neighboring larger plant in 1896. The factory was located at Újhegyi út 5–7, Budapest X. (At the intersection area near Gyömrői út in Kőbánya.) The deep clay quarry excavated for Guttmann's brickworks eventually filled with groundwater after quarrying ceased. There is there today a body of water serving as a public fishing pond. This body of water goes under various names. The official name is Újhegyi bányató. An unofficial name is "Guttmann-tó" (Guttmann Lake). And even Google Maps know it under both names. See here. On the shore of this lake is a snack bar called "Guttmann Teresz".

    We have files from 1891 that testify to the fact that "I. Guttmann" was also in Novi Sad where his company had a contract for the construction of earthworks at the Bácska embankment and at the Bogojevo railway line undertaken for the Royal Ministry of Agriculture. See, for example, here and here. On both files it states that Isaac is a "construction contractor" from "Novi Sad".

    In the remaining contracts Isaac is always a resident of Budapest. In 1894 Isaac Guttmann bought various large tracts of land in various parts of the Kingdom of Hungary. We assume the purpose of these purchases was for timber, mining and other materials. The first tract was in the Banat region of Temes-Krassó in present day Romania and cost 88,000 Hungarian florins, see here. We believe that the second tract was located near present-day Liptovský Mikuláš, a town in what is present day Slovakia, see here. It cost 20,000 florins. The third tract, seemingly in and around Szegény (in present-day Romania), cost 28,000 florins, see here. This total outlay in 1894 alone of 136,000 Hungarian florins was a significant sum. It is hard to say what that is in present day values. But according to Gemini: "Relative to modern income levels that economic weight equates to between $25 million to $40 million today".

    In 1895 Isaak Guttmann went into partnership with Traian Mebius, a landowner, and Jacob Haas and Ludwig Deutsch from Budapest. Guttmann and Mebius were the "general partners" (senior) while Haas and Deutsch were "limited partners". The company formed was called "Holzindustrie Kommanditgesellschaft Mebius, Guttmann & Co." The aim of the company was the exploitation of timber and logging rights in various locations in present day Romania. Guttmann contributed a capital investment of 60,000 florins, in cash. See here.

    We have a lengthy contract from 1898 where "I. Guttmann and Sons", represented by Hermann Guttmann, is selling some land that they own for 159,495 florins, see here. And in 1902 we have the purchase of some mining rights for the sum of 600,000 kronen (new currency that was the equivalent to 300,000 florin from previous years), see here. Finally in 1908 we have Hermann and Alfred, as sole representatives of I. Guttmann and Sons, dissolving the company, see here. Recall that Isaac died in January of 1906.

    This is obviously not the full picture. There seem to have been many other contracts, purchases and sales. We do know that the entire family inherited some land in Saint Lorinc near the Budapest airport after Katherine's death. At the time the family did not come to an agreement on what to do with it. Attempts were made, after the fall of communism, to receive compensation.


    4. Hermann and his offspring:

    As stated above, Hermann was born Hermann Eisner on 21 Mar 1865 in Vienna. He and his brother Alfred were formally adopted by Isaac Guttmann on 13 May 1894 and took the family name of Guttmann, see here. In 1895 Hermann married Margit Ungar. This is their wedding certificate. We were only able to decipher the year. Margit was born on 17 Oct 1876 in Gyékényes, Hungary. She was the daughter of Samuel Ungar and Paula Kremsier Ungar. Hermann's business interest were in both Budapest and Novi Sad. As noted above he and Alfred were partners with their father (step-father) from 1894 or 1895 until the latter's death. And in 1903 Hermann, brother Alfred and step-brother-in-law Leopold (Lipot) Frank (married to their step-sister Julie), together formed the company Guttman and Frank (Gutman i Frank) in Novi Sad. Each had a 1/3 interest therein. It was, or became, two companies. A construction company ("Građevinska firma Gutman i Frank") and a shipping and trading company ("Brodarstvo Gutman i Frank"). We will relate more on this later. But he and Margit were often to be found in Budapest. In fact these two cities were only about 300 km apart. Hermann and Margit had 4 children, all of whom were born in Budapest. They were Georgina (Gyorgyi) born 20 Aug 1899, Luiza (Lutzi) born about 1902, Rudolf (Rudi) born 26 Mar 1905 and Veronia (Vera) born 4 Dec 1910. We have here a copy of Hermann's Yugoslavian citizenship certificate from 1934. His height is listed as 1.68 m, his face is oval in shape, and his hair is grey. We know that Hermann owned and perhaps built a house at Vase Stajića 5 in Novi Sad. He later moved to Petra Zrinjskog 26 in the center of Novi Sad (today called Petra Drapsina). He and Rudi seem to have bought and built a house at Petra Zrinjskog 14. Hermann died on 4 Nov 1939 at the age of 74. The cause of his death is listed as arteriosclerosis cardiac paralysis. Hermann is buried in the Novi Sad Jewish Cemetery, (see here). Margit survived the war as, perhaps surprisingly, did all four of her children. Margit was for a while in Budapest, in hiding, with her grandson Peter (Rudi's son), Peter's mother Anna, and Anna's parents Bela and Ela Herzl. After the war Margit returned to Novi Sad and was initially with Rudi and his family (at Petra Zrinjskog 14). When Rudi and family came to Israel in Dec 1948, she went to be with her daughter Luiza. Margit came to Israel with her daughter Luiza and Luiza's family on 30 June 1949 on the ship "Radnik", see here. She lived with Luiza and family in Gedera, Israel. She died there in 1964 at the age of 88.

    Georgina (Gyorgi) was born on 20 Aug 1899 in Budapest. She was the eldest daughter of Hermann and Margit. On 30 Apr 1918 she married Dr. Armin (Hermann) Semsei in Budapest. (In Hungary the "Dr." title rarely means physician.) Armin was born on 30 Mar 1886 in the Pusztaszetlorinc neighbourhood of Budapest. Together they had two sons, Gabriel Stefan Semsei born 25 Jan 1919 in Budapest, and Tamas (Thomas) Otto Semsei born 16 Dec 1922 in Budapest. They lived at 54 Columbus utca in Budapest. It is possible that Gabriel was born Gabor. In the 1930's "Gabriel Semsei" became "Gabor Dacso". We know that Armin and Georgina (sometimes also called Stefania) were members of the Brit HaZohar B'Hungaria, i.e, the Zionist revisionist group in Hungary. As were their sons Gabriel and Tamas. All four members of the family survived the war. In 1946 we find Armin, Georgina, Tamas, and Gabor Dasco again living at 54 Columbus utca, see here. On forms to be found in the Arolsen Archives Georgina wrote that from "March 1944 to February 1945 I was hidden in a dark cellar room in Budapest", see here and here. On 25 April 1949 Armin died in Budapest of colon cancer, see here and here. He is listed therein as a "spirits and liqueur factory owner or wholesaler (or distillery owner)". Georgina remained in Budapest until 5 Jan 1950 when she emigrated from Hungary to Israel. She came on the ship she lists as the "Comijut" (perhaps it was the "Kommeniut"). In Israel she initially lived in Gedera, probably with her mother and/or one of her sisters. On 8 Aug 1952 she married Dr. Otto Kraus in Tel-Aviv. Dr. Otto Kraus was a chest physician. He was born in Austria on 8 Mar 1893. He was interred in Theresienstadt, survived, and immediately after the war became the official doctor for the American Joint Distribution Committee in Prague, see his obituary. Gabriel, under the name Gabor Dacso, emigrated to Sydney, Australia, arriving there on 16 Aug 1951. In March of 1955 Georgina, her husband Otto and her son Tamas also arrived in Sydney. Gabriel, who changed his family name back to Semsei, had a store, married and had a daughter. Sadly he died when his daughter was only 2 years old. Tamas, who was now officially Thomas, became a very successful tyre distributor. In fact he received an award from the Israel-Australia Chamber of Commerce in 1976, and the "Medal of the Order of Australia" from the Australian government in 1997 "For Service to Business Through Marketing in the Tyre Industry". Thomas married Eva Agota. We know nothing about any children. Otto died on 8 Feb 1972 , Gabriel on 17 Nov 1977, Georgina on 11 Jul 1983 and Thomas on 3 August 2008. They are all buried in the Macquarie Park Cemetery in Sydney.

    Luiza (Lutzi) was born about 1902 in Budapest. She first married Alexander Fenyvesi of whom we know nothing. It is said that he was from a prominent family in Transylvania. Together they had a son Stefan (Pista) born on 1 Feb 1925 in Oradea, Romania. Alexander and Luiza subsequently divorced. From what we can determine Stefan was brought up by his mother and they lived in both Subotica and Budapest. Stefan was a student. In about 1944 he was transported to KZ Mauthausen where he was put to work as a "machine fitter". He was murdered there on 18 Mar 1945. See this form from the Arolsen Archive, and the "Pages of Testimony" at the Yad VaShem website submitted by his mother and half-brother. Luiza married Arpad Hutter in about 1935. Arpad was born on 6 Apr 1891 in Visznek, Hungary. From what can be determined Arpad had been married at least twice previously. But this marrriage lasted. Arpad, via a daughter from a previous marriage, already had a granddaughter at the time of this marriage (both daughter and granddaughter were murdered in the Holocaust). Arpad came from a poor childhood, but studied and worked hard. He learnt the textile business and jointly owned and managed a textile company in Subotica with non-Jewish partners. Subotica is in Serbia, very close to the Hungarian border. It is about 100 km from Novi Sad and 200 km from Budapest. Until the end of WWI it was part of the Kingdom of Hungary. From the end of WWI it was in Yugoslavia. Their son Tomislav (called Tommy) was born on 12 May 1936 in Budapest. (We assume Luiza went to her parents home to give birth.) Arpad's business prospered and allowed the family to maintain a proper upper-middle class life in Subotica. The family had 3 domestic workers, a car and a driver, as well as a summer place near Budapest. Luiza went to take "the cure" from time to time. Tommy tells about being in first grade with all the children of his neighbourhood and that he had both Jewish and non-Jewish friends. But by the second grade he was in a school only with Jewish children. As WWII progressed Tommy and Luiza were moved into a Ghetto in Subotica, while Arpad was sent to a nearby work camp, but came home every few weeks. On 16 June 1944 Luiza and Tommy were shipped by cattle cars to the Strasshof camp near Vienna, having been joined by Arpad along the way. They were the lucky ones. Strasshof was not an extermination camp, see here. Jews were being held there for a possible exchange by the Nazis of Jews for material. Nonetheless it was a prison camp and there were major hygiene and food problems. Together with some 50-60 other Hungarian Jews they were then moved to a nearby village where the adults worked digging canals near the Danube. This was probably the Ulrichskirchen Camp. The children worked in the kitchen and had other duties. The family was there for almost a full year. Tommy recalled how some locals left them potatoes and beet sugar. They were liberated on 9 May 1945. Arpad, Luiza and Tommy had survived the war. Stefan had not. They immigrated to Israel arriving on 30 June 1949 on the ship "Radnik", see here. They lived in Gedera where they are now all buried. When Tommy arrived in Israel he was initially sent by himself to a kibbutz. However he was diagnosed with tuberculosis and spent a lengthy period of time at a sanatorium in Nes Ziona. Due to this fact he was also not conscripted into the armed forces. Tommy became a water expert and worked for the Makorot company for over 40 years. He continued to live in Gedera on the same farm as his parents. He married Aliza nee Bonomo (born in 1946), and together they had 4 children and, at last count, 13 grandchildren. Tommy's one hour interview on his life can be found on YouTube here. It is in Hebrew. Tommy died in January 2013.

    Rudolf (Rudi) was born on 26 Mar 1905 in Budapest. He married Anna Herzl who was born 11 Oct 1911 in Budapest. Here is a wedding photo. We believe they lived and worked in Novi Sad, but often went to Budapest. Anna and Rudi's son Peter was born on 12 Feb 1938 in Budapest. Anna's parents were Bela (Adelbert) Herzl (1881 Ada - 1959 Budapest) and Ela Handler Herzl (1881 Mosorin - 1965 Budapest). As the only son it seems that Rudi was active in the family business "Gutman i Frank". During the early war years Yugoslavia was at peace, until it was invaded and partitioned by the Axis powers (Germany, Italy, Hungary, and Bulgaria) in the spring of 1941. In these early war years efforts were made by various Jewish organizations to ship Jews to safety via Yugoslavia. Rudi's cousin Laci Guttmann (see Section 3) was very much involved in these efforts. But Rudi and others associated with the company "Gutman i Frank" also helped, see this translation of part of an article by Dr. Francia Ofner where Rudi is mentioned twice. The fact that Rudi had a car was very useful for transporting materials and food. Peter with his mother, his Herzl grandparents and Rudi's mother Margit were in Budapest, where it was safer for the first period of the war. Sadly at some time in 1943 Anna died of a brain tumor. Moreover at some point, probably after Germany took over the Hungarian government in 1944, they went into hiding. They spent months in the basement of a worker of Bela Herzl. During this period Rudi was sent to a work camp, escaped, and joined the Yugoslav (Tito) partisans (the communist-led anti-fascist resistance to the Axis powers in occupied Yugoslavia during World War II). A family story is that Rudi twice had to prove he was not a Hungarian soldier. Once when he went to join the Yugoslav partisans, and then when he came back to Novi Sad after the war and was confronted by the Russian army. Both times he said he was a Jew and dropped his pants. He passed muster.  In the list of Yugoslav Jews who survived the war we find, at the same address Petra Zrinjskog 14 in Novi Sad, Rudi, his mother Margit, his cousin Imre (Mirko) along with Imre's wife and daughter, and his in-laws Bela and Ela Herzl (see here and also here). Rudi remarried, probably in 1946. His new wife was Katalin Bierman Lempel. She was widowed, and the mother of Tommy Lempel who became, in Israel, Tommy Lapid, a very well-known journalist and politician. It is said that Bela and Ela Herzl opposed the marriage, but to no avail. They all lived together in Novi Sad where Rudi had set up a business. He owned a small shipyard. In late 1948 Jews were permitted, for a short period of time, to leave Yugoslavia. Rudi, Katalin, Peter and Tommy all left. They arrived in Haifa on 26 Dec 1948, on the boat "Dromit", see here, formerly named "Kefalos". (Also there are his cousin Imre and Imre's daughter Suzana.) Rudi retrieved money he had sent to England. In Tel-Aviv he established a mushroom growing business that flourished until it did not. Rudi died on 10 Dec 1954 from throat cancer. Katalin remarried. She died on 7 July 1973. On arrival in Israel, Peter was almost immediately sent to live on Kibbutz Kfar Szold. He was all of ten years old, a child who had already been through the Holocaust, lost his mother, and in a new country with a language he did not speak. It is said that his father and step-mother only came to visit him once every two or three months. At some point Peter moved to another kibbutz that he eventually left. He worked in the hotel industry, and then later for Bank Hapoalim. He married Ella Wertheim (she was born in 1946) and they had 3 children. Peter died on 29 Jul 2024 of cancer. He is buried in Tel-Aviv. Here is a translation of the Eulogy given by Tommy Lapid's son Yair Lapid at Peter's funeral. And here is a photo of Peter and his older stepbrother Tommy Lapid. Peter is on our right. What we have reported on here is mainly based on what we have been told by Peter's children, and on what appeared in the book "Memories After My Death: The Story of My Father, Joseph 'Tommy' Lapid", a biography of Tommy Lapid written in autobiographical form by his son Yair Lapid.

    Veronica (Vera) was born on 4 Dec 1910 in Budapest. She married Alfred (Fredi) Weiss on 5 Oct 1930 in Budapest. Fredi was born on 10 Apr 1900 in Bánovce nad Bu, Slovakia. They had two daughters. Hella, who was born on 10 Aug 1932 in Vienna. Here is her birth certificate. And Shoshana (Susi), who was born on 15 Nov 1936 in Budapest. Fredi ran (but did not own) a farm in the small village of Kamanova near Topolcany, Slovakia. For much of the war they were unmolested. In fact they were issued a "permission document" which said that what Fredi and Vera were doing, namely farming, was essential to the Slovak economy, and so the family could not and should not be shipped out. This kept them safe, until it did not. They were the only Jews in the village. And they seem to have gotten on well with their neighbours. This situation continued until September of 1944. But then they had to go into hiding. In fact they hid in their own house, in a cellar, during the day. According to Hella they played a lot of cards and were able to get out at night. However at some point someone informed on them to the Germans. The family was sent to the Sered camp where Fredi was beaten, and Hella was almost raped. It was already 1945. Finally on 12 Mar 1945 they were transported to Theresienstadt. And at some later date they were then put on a transport to an extermination camp. But the train was bombed and some people in the forward carriages were killed. Fortunately they were near the end of the train. The commander had no idea as to what to do, and so brought them back to Theresienstadt. In May 1945 Theresienstadt was liberated by the Russians. The family returned to Kamanova. The house had been ransacked. But their domestic help had come every so often to collect their things, which they then got back. The family stayed on in Slovakia for a few years. They even "Slovakizied" their family name from Weiss to Verny in 1946, see here. In 1955 in Israel they officially changed it back to Weiss. Hella Weiss came to Israel in Feb 1949 with Aliyat HaNoar and HaShomer Hatzair. She enlisted in the army and became a nurse. Vera and Fredi arrived in Israel with Shoshana (Susi) in Nov 1949. They went to Gedera because Fredi had relatives there. They initially rented a farm. But they then bought it together with Margit, Lutzi, Arpad and Tommy, partially with money that the family had managed to deposit in Switzerland before the war. In addition, in the 1950's Vera and Lutzi ran a laundry. Eventually they also received some German reparations. Fredi died on 15 Nov 1980 and is buried in Gedera, while Vera died on 8 Apr 1984 and is also buried in Gedera. Hella met and married Marcel (Mordechai) Kornblum on 21 Mar 1954, see here. Marcel was born 12 Jun 1923 in Krakow. He had had a difficult war. Was in the camps and contracted tuberculosis that affected him his entire life. In Israel he eventually became a police officer. Together Hella and Mordechai had two sons and a daughter. For many years they lived in Petach Tikva. But in 1973 they transferred to Jerusalem. Mordechai died on 14 Feb 2005 and Hella died on 26 Feb 2018. Both are buried in Jerusalem. Shoshana married Reuven Drori (Mauskopf) who was born on 19 Aug 1936 in Nitra, Slovakia. They were members of Kibbutz Dan. On shlichut in New York in the early 1980s it was discovered that Shoshana had cancer. They returned to Kibbutz Dan where Shoshana died on 8 Feb 1983. Together they had a son, who died as a baby, and three girls. None of Fredi and Vera's descendants lived in Gedera. So when Vera died the Hutter family bought the Weiss family's part of the farm. (The farm is all of one and a half dunam, which is about a third of an acre.) Hella memories were recorded, in Hebrew, here.

    5. Alfred and his offspring:

    Alfred was born on 19 Feb 1866 in Vienna, and with his brother Hermann, was formally adopted by Isaac Guttmann and so took the family name Guttmann. Alfred married Eugenia (Jenny) Stein on 25 Mar 1900 in Baja, Hungary. Here is their marriage certificate. Jenny was born on 13 Feb 1881 in Katymar (not too far from Baja). Together they had three sons, all born in Budapest. They were Imre (Mirko) born 2 Mar 1901, Pal (Palve) born 29 Sep 1902, and Laszlo (Ladislav, Laci) born many years later on 5 Jan 1914. In early 1923 Alfred and Jenny made a will with their notary in Budapest, see here and here for an English translation. It holds no surprises, leaving the estate to be divided among their children, while the surviving spouse has the right to use, live in, and profit from all of the property in the estate for the rest of his/her life. It was known that Alfred was terminally ill, and in fact he died on 18 Oct 1925 "after a long illness". Here is the announcement of his death as it appeared in Pester Lloyd. This announcement says that he is to be buried in Budapest, but he is also listed on the same gravestone as his wife who is buried in Novi Sad. (Also listed on that gravestone is his son Palve (Pal) who was murdered in the Holocaust in the Ukraine.) His wife Jenny died on 4 Aug 1935 in Novi Sad. Here is data from JewishGen. In addition, we have a letter from the Jewish community in Novi Sad that states that just prior to the war the house at Vojvođanska 5, Novi Sad, was jointly owned by the three sons. So this was most probably where Alfred and Jenny lived. Moreover Alfred initiated construction on a building called "Vila Gutman" which still stands in Novi Sad today at Vase Stajica 3. See, for example, here. It is truly outstanding. It was built in 1925 and 1926 and designed by the architect Dragutin Maslac.

    Imre (Mirko) was the oldest son of Alfred and Jenny. He was born on 2 Mar 1901 in Budapest. Here is his birth registration. We know that he married Agnes (Agneza) Sar. She was born on 26 Nov 1913 in Szeged, Hungary. They had a daughter Suzana who was born 4 Mar 1937 in Subotica, Serbia. On various lists Imre is recorded with the title "Ing." But some of these lists give his occupation as an architect. It seems very probable that Imre was one of the main directors of the firm "Gutman i Frank". There are two "Vila Gutman" in Novi Sad, see above. Imre Guttman is associated with the Vila Gutman that was built in 1936 and stands at Ulica Pavla Simica 9. This highly pared-down modernist building was built by Imre Gutman and designed by the architect Danilo Kaćanski. Imre, Agnes and Suzana survived the war. We do not know how or where. They are on the list of Jewish survivors after the war living with Rudi and Margit at Petra Zrinjskog 14 in Novi Sad. However on one of these lists Suzana is not listed with her parents but is listed in the section "NOVI SAD later arrived". See here and here. Recall that she was all of 8 years old when the war ended. Possibly she was hidden somewhere with someone. On the ship "Dromit" that arrived in Haifa on 26 Dec 1948, see the passenger list we have Imre and his daughter Suzana, but no wife Agnes. (They are there with Rudi, his wife, son and stepson.) We have no idea as to what they did or where they lived in Israel. It seems that Imre died in Israel on 20 Jan 1956. He is buried in the Kiryat Shaul cemetery in Tel-Aviv. That this is our Imre is testified to by notes left by his youngest brother. We believe that Suzana married someone with the family name of Broder. We have been unable to trace her.

    Pal (Palve) was born 29 Sep 1902 in Budapest. Here is his birth registration. He married Lydia Loschitz. She was born in Novi Sad on 26 May 1909. Their daughter Katarina Eva Guttman was born in Novi Sad on 21 Feb 1933. Theirs is a tragic story. Pal was conscripted into the labor battalion of the Hungarian army. Most of those conscripted perished or were murdered. Pal was shot and killed by a Hungarian Nazi collaborator on 28 Feb 1943 in Khrenovoye, Ukraine. His wife and daughter lived in Budapest. They were taken to the Danube and shot by Hungarian Nazi collaborators on 15 Jan 1945. The source for this information are the three "Pages of Testimony" submitted by Pal's younger brother Laszlo (Ladislav, Laci) in 1998. See here, here, and here.

    Laszlo (Ladislav, Laci) was born on 5 Jan 1914 in Budapest. He was the youngest child of Alfred and Jenny. Here is his birth certificate. Over the years he went by various first names, e.g. Laszlo, Ladislav, Laci, Yitzhak, Guci, and variants thereof. As he often signed himself Laci, we will use that name as our default. Much, but not all, of what we report here is taken from Laci's own writings. Laci, as noted, was born in Budapest. He first lived in the village of Kamenica (today called Sremska Kamenica) adjacent to Novi Sad. He was taken often to Vienna and Budapest. While still young his father built a house in Novi Sad, and they moved there. He attended Gymnasia (secondary school) in Novi Sad from where he matriculated. His school friends called him “Guci”, and this also became his nom-de-guerre. He was a Zionist from an early age. In 1927, as a high-school student, he was a member of the organization “Ivriyah” in Novi Sad and active on behalf of the Jewish National Fund (KKL). His father died when he was 11 years old, but the family had money and Laci attended the University of Zagreb where he studied pharmacy and chemistry, eventually obtaining a degree in pharmacy. While at university Laci became a member of the Zionist Academic Club. At the head of this student organization was his friend Francia Ofner, also from Novi Sad, who was a law student at Zagreb University. In the autumn of 1933, Isidor Schoor, Viktor Stark, Francia Ofner, Laci Guttman and others founded the Zionist youth movement “Beitar” in Yugoslavia. These people, including Laci Guttman were, one after the other, the leaders (Natziv) of Beitar in Yugoslavia. Laci's tenure was in the years 1935-1938. He was on the editorial board of the Yugoslav Beitar journal where he wrote articles also under the pseudonym "Yitzhak ShemTov".

    When Beitar in the various countries became involved in the illegal aliya (Jewish immigration to Palestine), Laci was very active in this matter. A word of explanation. Until the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941, the country served as a vital escape route for Jewish refugees. And Novi Sad was the first serious port on the Danube downstream from Hungary. According to Francia Ofner, see this translation of an article he later wrote: "We left shipping problems to Guci. Guci was Laci Guttmann, my successor as Natziv of Beitar. Mirko Popper, Rudi Guttmann and others in the shipping company "Guttmann and Frank" worked with Guci in that area." As one source noted he used both his maritime knowledge and his deep commitment to Zionist youth movements to support clandestine migration to Palestine. From the late 1930s Guci was heavily involved in organising escape routes across Yugoslavia. Working alongside many, many others, he helped procure visas, secure safe passage, and arrange food, clothing, and coal for refugees travelling down the Danube or across the Adriatic. Between 1939 and 1940 alone, he and his associates assisted as many as 20,000 Jewish refugees from Poland, Austria, Hungary, and elsewhere. His family’s shipping connections allowed him to coordinate the movement of refugees on Danube vessels such as the Saturnus, Krein, Erzsébet Királyné, Minerva, and Tuln, which carried migrants from Vienna, Bratislava, and Budapest toward the Black Sea. One of his most famous operations took place in 1938, when he helped organise the boarding of nearly two thousand refugees onto the ship Draga, sailing under a Panamanian flag from the port of Fiume (Rijeka). The refugees in two groups, one from Vienna and one from Poland, were moved secretly through Yugoslavia to Susak and then across the narrow footbridge into Italian territory. When Yugoslav authorities forbade local Jewish youth from joining the group, he found an alternative route, sending them by train to Athens so they could embark at Piraeus.

    Laci’s activities inevitably attracted the attention of the authorities. He was first arrested by the Yugoslav authorities in 1938, spending around two months in Belgrade’s central police prison and the notorious Glavnjaci facility. Even in custody he continued his advocacy, writing a twenty‑page account of Jewish suffering and the aims of the Aliyah movement, which he handed to interrogators in an attempt to win sympathy. As a pharmacist he worked in Novi Sad and Budapest. In the autumn of 1942, he was arrested in Budapest and transferred to Novi Sad because of anti-fascist activity. He was tortured in the “Armia” building in Novi Sad for nearly a month. He was then transferred to the military‑political prison on Margit Körút in Budapest, the same prison where Hannah Senesh was later executed. He spent eleven months there before being released in October 1943.

    The short respite gave him the opportunity to marry Magda (Miriam) Bohm. They married on 21 Nov 1943 in Budapest. Magda was born on 19 May 1920 in Budapest. Her father's family was originally from Novi Sad. Magda's mother died soon after her birth and Magda was initially sent to live with her paternal grandparents in Novi Sad. Her father Ludwig Bohm studied dentistry in Germany. He had a dental office and a dental laboratory. When Ludwig remarried, Magda returned to Budapest. But the family often visited Novi Sad. After finishing secondary school Magda became a dental technician. Magda faithfully supported Laci even before their marriage, visiting him in various prisons/camps. According to Francia Ofner and his wife, who were in Istanbul during this period, at some time, the dates are unclear, papers were sent from abroad that would have permitted Laci and Magda to legally emigrate to Palestine. These papers never reached Laci and Magda, and were used by others. A long oral interview with Francia Ofner and his wife about life in Novi Sad before the war, their experiences during the war, with some details of what we have related above can be found here.

    In June of 1944 the Jews of Budapest were moved into the Ghetto. During the night of 25-26 June British airplanes bombed Budapest and the house where Magda was living suffered a direct hit. Magda was in the basement of the house. Of the 24 people who were buried in that basement only three survived. Among them was Madga. She was dug out of the ruins after 8 hours but had suffered a very serious leg injury and could not walk. After a few days she was transferred to the Jewish hospital and from there, on a stretcher, and on the Kasztner train (see below), to the Bergen-Belsen concentration camp. In the camp, due to her injuries, she could not get out of bed for 4 months, and she was in constant fear that she would be shot. At the beginning of January 1944 Laci was again arrested (for the third time). This time he was conscripted into the Jewish labor battalion, within the framework of the Hungarian army. In June 1944 he was at the Jewish forced labor camp that was a brick factory in the city of Kecskemét, Hungary. From there he was sent to Bergen-Belsen.

    In July 1944, both Laci and Magda managed to get a place on the Kasztner train.  Places on the train were much sought after and Laci/Magda may well have paid for their places (as their relative Malvine Kremsier did).  The train was meant to go to Palestine via Spain, according to the negotiation between Rudolf Kasztner and the Nazis, but was instead diverted to Bergen Belsen in northern Germany.  There were around 1600 people on the train.  One group was released after a few weeks, the other (including Laci and Magda) was sent to Switzerland arriving on 7 December 1944. Laci never mentions this in his various writings, but it is documented, see here. In Switzerland Laci was active in Beitar affairs. There is this file from Beit Jabotinsky containing correspondence between himself and Reuven Hecht (who calls him Guci).

    In late August of 1945 Laci and Magda were transferred to Italy. They were then somehow smuggled onto the ship "Ville d'Oran" that left the port of Taranto. This was a legitimate ship tranporting goods and people in the chaos after WWII. It docked at Haifa on 2 Sept 1945, see here. It had many "legal" immigrants, i.e., passengers with "certificates". But the Mossad LeAliyah Bet (the underground branch of the Jewish leadership in Palestine) and the Brihah ("Flight") organization through a combination of covert organizing, bribed authorities, and false documentation, smuggled some 150 passengers, without certificates, onto the Ville d'Oran. When the ship docked in Haifa these "illegals" were detained and sent to the Atlit Detention Camp for five to six weeks, from September 2, 1945, to October 9, 1945, when they were freed in a famous Palmach raid. Whether Laci and Magda were freed in this raid is not clear, as Magda had limited mobility.

    Laci and Magda remained in the Haifa area. Initially Laci was active in Etzel (Irgun Zvai Leumi). Within the framework of the Hagannah he helped organize a blood bank in an emergency hospital that was established in Haifa. This work continued during the War of Independence, and morphed into work with the blood bank of the Magen David Adom in Haifa. He then worked as a scientific advisor in various pharmaceutical companies. He had other jobs. Eventually he finished studies at the Technion as a "special librarian". This allowed him to work for 13 years as a bibliograph and library advisor in the main Haifa library. Magda had operations that enabled her to walk again. She held a variety of jobs. Her father Ludwig and his wife arrived in Israel from Yugoslavia in December 1948. Ludwig lived in Afula where he worked in the dental profession. He died in 1964. Magda and Laci's daughter Irith was born on July 4, 1951. Magda died on Apr 28, 1981 and Laci died on Dec. 14, 1999, see here. Their daughter Irith married and has two daughters, born in 1987 and 1990. She and her family live in the Haifa area.

    There are two files in the Entov Collection of the National Library in Jerusalem deposited there by Laci. This first file tells us a lot about his life. It also contains a set of tributes to Laci on his 80th birthday, that includes profuse praise for his wartime rescue activities. The second file is more about Beitar in Yugoslavia. There are also files at Beit Jabotinsky in Tel-Aviv. But we was only able to access this file, mentioned previously, containing correspondence between Reuven Hecht and Laci while the latter was in Switzerland in 1945. Laci's writings and archival papers preserve the story of a rather remarkable yet largely forgotten person.
